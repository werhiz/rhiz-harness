# Closeout: what the review found, what landed, and what the score is now

Date: 2026-08-21
Companions: `2026-08-20-stack-review.md` (defects), `2026-08-20-integration-state.md` (assembly)

The stack review ended with a number: **3.5 / 10** on the question *would I trust this Harness to modify my production repository.* That number is now stale in both directions, and a stale verdict at the top of a review is worse than no verdict, because it gets quoted.

## What actually changed

`main` at `0743288`: **318 tests passing, 11 guards proven falsifiable, portable boundary and barrel checks green.** On the morning of 2026-08-20 `main` was eight documents and an empty file named `tmp`, with twelve open pull requests and nothing merged.

Closed with evidence, each verified against `main` rather than inferred from a merged PR:

Added rather than fixed: a falsifiability gate (#35) that requires every declared guard to be provably load bearing, and which found an enforcement in the Verify engine that no test had ever covered.

## The revised number: 6 / 10

**Up from 3.5** because the two structural P0s are genuinely closed. Work now happens in the directory the Board believes it happens in, and workspace identity now covers the bytes that execute rather than git's opinion of them. Those were the two failures that made every downstream guarantee decorative, and they are gone. The falsifiability gate makes the class of defect that produced them harder to reintroduce.

**Not higher, for one reason.** #11 is open and untouched: there is no OS containment. A worker runs with the operator's uid, its own `PATH`, and unrestricted filesystem and network access. Nothing prevents a write to `~/.ssh` or to the parent repository's `.git/config`. Guard landed and still has zero call sites (#40), so no tool call is evaluated by anything.

6 means: **trust it against a scratch clone, not against a repository you cannot afford to lose.** The honest gap between 6 and 9.5 is now almost entirely one item, where in the original review it was ten.

## What today actually taught, beyond the patches

**Measuring an integration cost is not paying it.** The assembly probe measured the exact cost of landing the fan: three barrel conflicts, one real `schemas.ts` union, two stale fixtures. `main` was then broken twice, by both of those fixtures, by the same person holding the list. Verifying after each merge caught them within minutes; verifying the merge result before merging would have caught them at zero.

**A test that passes proves the code does something, never that the guard is why.** Every defect in the review was that shape. So was a mutation during the review itself, which silently failed to apply and produced a green run that was nearly reported as proof. That is what the falsifiability gate exists to make impossible, and the gate found a real hole on its first run.

**Assertions drift toward the code.** `assert.notEqual(decision, "allow")` passed review, passed CI, and was satisfied by two materially different products. Caught by an independent reader, not by the suite (#39).

**A closed issue is a claim.** Merging #30 auto-closed #11, the containment P0 that #30 explicitly does not address. Nothing was wrong with the code; the tracker simply began asserting that the gate had been met. Reopened with evidence.

## Open, ranked

- **#40 (P1)** Guard has no call sites, and is currently unreachable rather than merely unwired.
- **#32 (P1)** Falsifiability is opt-in, and authority exceptions are invisible in receipts and unreconstructible from the Ledger.

- **#21, #22** the P2 and P3 rollups.

## The one thing to carry forward

The system is now honest about what it does. That is a different property from being safe, and the distance between them is #11.
