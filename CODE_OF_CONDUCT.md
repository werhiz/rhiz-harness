# Rhiz Harness Code of Conduct

Rhiz Harness is built through rigorous disagreement, evidence, and shared responsibility. Participation in this project requires professional conduct toward contributors, users, maintainers, and people affected by the software.

## Expected conduct

Contributors should:

- critique ideas, code, evidence, and decisions rather than attacking people;
- make technical disagreements specific and reproducible;
- acknowledge uncertainty and correct claims when evidence changes;
- give credit for work and preserve provenance;
- respect privacy, credentials, proprietary information, and responsible-disclosure boundaries;
- avoid harassment, threats, intimidation, discrimination, or targeted humiliation;
- avoid sexualized conduct or unwelcome personal attention in project spaces;
- avoid deliberately disrupting collaboration, reviews, issue tracking, or release processes;
- treat less-experienced contributors as collaborators whose work should be reviewed clearly and fairly;
- use the project's architecture and proof processes to resolve technical disputes rather than authority by persistence or volume.

## Review culture

Strong review is welcome. A useful review explains the defect, its impact, and the evidence needed to resolve it.

Statements such as "this is unsafe," "this breaks the architecture," or "this does not work" should be tied to a concrete boundary, reproduction, test, ADR, contract, or other evidence whenever possible.

Reviewers may block work that fails a security, correctness, provenance, or architectural requirement. Blocking a change is not permission to demean its author.

## Scope

This policy applies in project-controlled spaces including:

- GitHub issues, pull requests, discussions, and reviews;
- project chat or collaboration channels;
- community events and calls held on behalf of the project;
- public interactions where a participant is clearly representing the Rhiz Harness project.

## Reporting conduct concerns

During private dogfood, report conduct concerns privately to a repository maintainer through the established project channel. Do not publish private personal information as part of a report.

Maintainers handling a report should limit information to the people needed to evaluate and resolve it, while preserving enough evidence to make a fair decision.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and history, maintainers may:

- ask for a correction or apology;
- remove or edit project-controlled content where appropriate;
- limit participation in a thread or review;
- temporarily restrict project access;
- permanently remove a participant from project-controlled spaces.

Serious threats, harassment, doxxing, credential abuse, or deliberate security exploitation may result in immediate restriction.

## Maintainer responsibility

Maintainers are subject to the same standard. Enforcement decisions should be based on documented conduct rather than technical disagreement, status, personal relationships, or popularity.

When a maintainer is materially involved in a conduct dispute, another maintainer should handle the decision where practical.

## Purpose

The standard is simple: rigorous work, clear evidence, respect for people, and accountability for consequences.
